# Figure captions (draft)

**Figure 1.** UV–Vis absorption of 20 nm polystyrene (PS) nanoparticles in water. (a) Absorbance spectra at 10 µg/mL, 0.5 µg/mL and 1 ng/mL (mean of n = 3, shaded ± SD; logarithmic y-axis). (b) PS absorption spectrum obtained by subtracting the 1 ng/mL spectrum (used as background) from the 10 µg/mL spectrum, showing the shoulder at ~212 nm and the phenyl B-band (π→π*) with vibronic structure at 253, 262 and 269 nm (inset). (c) Second-derivative spectra (Savitzky–Golay, 11-point window, 3rd-order polynomial). Minima at 262 and 269 nm appear only at 10 µg/mL. Data below 205 nm (A > 2) are not shown.

**Figure 2.** Optical absorption edge of 20 nm PS nanoparticles. (a) Absorbance versus photon energy (E = 1239.84/λ). (b, c) Tauc plots for direct allowed, (αhν)², and indirect allowed, (αhν)^1/2, transitions, with α = 2.303·ΔA/l (l = 1 cm) from the background-subtracted 10 µg/mL spectrum. Solid lines are linear fits to the steepest part of the absorption edge (filled symbols). Their x-intercepts give E_g = 5.48 eV (direct) and 5.28 eV (indirect).

**Figure 3.** Background-subtracted absorbance of PS nanoparticles versus concentration at (a) 220 nm and (b) 262 nm. Error bars show ± SD of replicate measurements (1 ng/mL, n = 3). The shaded band is ±3σ of the 1 ng/mL replicates, which defines the detection threshold.

**Figure S1.** Reproducibility of the 1 ng/mL measurements. (a) Three replicate spectra and their mean. (b) Standard deviation at each wavelength.

**Figure S2.** Absorbance versus wavelength on log–log axes. The slopes fitted between 280 and 400 nm (−1.09 for 10 µg/mL, about −0.5 for the others) are far from the −4 expected for Rayleigh scattering, so particle scattering contributes little to the extinction.

## Methods text (draft)

The 20 nm PS stock (25 mg/mL in water containing 200 ppm SDS) was serially diluted in ultrapure water. UV–Vis spectra were recorded on a PerkinElmer Lambda 950 from 200 to 400 nm in 1 nm steps, using a 1 cm quartz cuvette. Because the SDS was diluted together with the particles, the residual SDS was ≤ 0.08 µg/mL at all working concentrations and contributed no measurable absorbance. The 1 ng/mL dilution, whose expected PS absorbance is negligible (~5 × 10⁻⁶ at 262 nm), was therefore used as the background reference.
